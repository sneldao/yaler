# Validation and onboarding

**Horizon:** post-Kiro / customer pilot. Do not run this interview programme before the 23 August submission. The Kiro demo uses labelled synthetic suppliers and one reproducible test mission. See [SCOPE.md](SCOPE.md) for the slice that ships first.

## Goal

Recruit enough London participants to test whether Yaler can coordinate a real operational mission, without pretending that a hackathon demo is already a live marketplace.

## Initial participant profile

### Demand side

Look for:

- Independent cafés
- Small restaurants
- Takeaways
- Bakeries
- Food trucks
- Small hospitality groups with several locations

The best early demand-side participant has experienced an urgent equipment, cleaning, maintenance, or supplier problem recently and still coordinates it through calls, WhatsApp, email, or spreadsheets.

### Supply side

Look for:

- Commercial refrigeration providers
- Commercial dishwasher or catering-equipment repairers
- Extraction and cleaning providers
- Maintenance companies
- Equipment replacement suppliers
- Non-regulated facilities-service providers

Start with providers who already serve small businesses and can describe a service area and response window.

## Interview questions: demand side

1. Tell me about the last operational issue that disrupted service.
2. How did you find someone to solve it?
3. How many providers did you contact?
4. What information did providers ask for repeatedly?
5. What made you trust or distrust a provider?
6. How did you compare quotes or arrival times?
7. What happened when the provider was late or unavailable?
8. What would you be comfortable delegating to software?
9. What would always require your approval?
10. Would a verified completion receipt be useful to you?

Do not pitch Yaler before understanding the current workflow.

## Interview questions: supply side

1. What kinds of jobs do you want more of?
2. What makes an incoming request well-scoped?
3. What information is usually missing?
4. How do you communicate availability and price?
5. What causes wasted visits or unqualified leads?
6. What would make you trust a buyer-side agent?
7. Would you respond to structured job requests from an agent?
8. What evidence can you provide after completion?
9. What tasks should never be accepted autonomously?
10. Would a verified record of completed work help win repeat business?

## Onboarding flow

### Buyer

1. Create a lightweight business profile.
2. Describe one recent or current operational mission.
3. Set budget, deadline, radius, and approval preferences.
4. Review the generated mandate.
5. Run a test mission.
6. Review the proof receipt.
7. Give feedback.

### Supplier

1. Create a supplier profile.
2. Select capabilities and service area.
3. State availability and typical response time.
4. Upload or enter permissioned evidence of capability.
5. Receive a test offer request.
6. Respond with terms.
7. Review how the agent represents the offer.

## Pilot safety

- Use synthetic missions unless a participant explicitly authorizes a real one.
- Do not collect unnecessary personal information.
- Do not autonomously dispatch gas, electrical, structural, or other regulated work.
- Do not take payment details.
- Do not publish business names or evidence without permission.
- Clearly label simulated providers and missions.
- Keep a human operator available.

## Fast onboarding target

**Kiro submission (now):** 3 labelled synthetic supplier agents, 1 completed test mission, 1 documented exception or reroute. No live interviews required.

**Horizon 2 pilot, after submission, aim for:**

- 5 demand-side conversations
- 5 supply-side conversations
- 3 curated real supplier profiles in the registry
- 1 demand-side test profile
- 1 completed test mission with a human operator on standby
- 1 documented exception or reroute

These numbers are for learning and evidence, not claims of traction.

## What to learn

The biggest unknowns are:

- Whether buyers value speed, price, or reliability most.
- Whether suppliers trust agent-mediated requests.
- Whether evidence can be collected without excessive effort.
- Whether a buyer will delegate a second mission.
- Whether a proof receipt creates referrals.

The mission loop should change based on these conversations.
